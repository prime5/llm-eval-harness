One of the most valuable things you can do when young is to figure out what you love to work on. If you find it, you're lucky. Most people don't, and they spend their lives in work that's okay but not great.

Working hard is different from working long hours. Working hard means pushing yourself to do better work, not just more work. The person who works 12 hours on autopilot is not working harder than the person who works 6 hours at the edge of their ability.

Different kinds of work require different kinds of hard work. Mathematical work requires concentrated, uninterrupted attention. Writing requires the same, plus a willingness to be honest. Managing people requires a different kind of energy — more social, more reactive, but still genuinely hard in its own way.

One of the most important things to figure out is when to work on what. Not all hours are equal. Most people have certain hours when they're mentally sharpest. Those hours should go to the most demanding work. Administrative tasks, email, and meetings can fill the less sharp hours.

Hard work has a compounding effect. People who work hard for years build skills and knowledge that make future hard work more productive. The advantage of starting to work hard young is that you have decades for this compounding to accumulate.

There's a difference between difficult work and important work. The most valuable hard work is at the intersection: work that is difficult and that matters. Doing hard things that don't matter is not the path to great outcomes.

Procrastination is a signal. When you're avoiding work, ask yourself whether the avoidance is because the work is unpleasant or because something is genuinely wrong — maybe you're doing the wrong thing, or you're missing something important, or you're tired and need rest. Each of these calls for a different response.

Rest is part of working hard. People who never rest eventually burn out or produce declining quality work. Strategic rest — real rest, not distracted pseudo-rest — lets you come back to hard work refreshed. Sleep is particularly important and is not something you can cheat sustainably.

The people who seem to work the hardest often seem to be enjoying themselves. This is not a coincidence. When work is aligned with interest, the effort feels less effortful. The goal is not to endure the most suffering; the goal is to find work where hard effort feels like forward progress.
