Writing is a powerful tool for thinking. When you write about something, you force yourself to articulate ideas that previously existed only as vague intuitions. The act of writing makes the holes in your thinking visible.

A blank page is honest. You can't fake understanding when you're writing. You either know what you think or you discover that you don't. That's the value of writing even when no one will read it.

The most important thing about writing is that it reveals your actual thinking, not the thinking you thought you had. Many people are surprised to find, when they write, that their ideas are less developed than they believed.

Good writing requires good thinking. You cannot write clearly about something you don't understand clearly. This is why writing is so useful as a test of comprehension. If you can explain something simply in writing, you understand it. If you can't, you don't.

Rewriting is thinking. The first draft captures what you think you know. The revisions capture what you actually learn. Writers who skip revision are leaving most of the value of writing on the table.

Clarity in writing comes from clarity of purpose. Before writing anything longer than a paragraph, you should be able to state in one sentence what you're trying to say and why someone should care. If you can't do this, you're not ready to write.

Short sentences are usually better than long ones. Long sentences tend to obscure the relationship between ideas. Short sentences force you to be explicit about those relationships. But mechanical shortness is not the goal — precision is.

The best essays surprise even the writer. They start with a question rather than an answer. The writing is a process of discovery, not a process of transcription. If you already know everything you're going to say before you write it, the essay is unlikely to be interesting.

Reading good writing makes you a better writer. Not because you copy it, but because it raises your standard for what writing can be. You can't aim for something you've never seen.

The hardest thing in writing is to be honest. To say things that are actually true, that you actually believe, rather than things that sound good or that other people want to hear. Most writing is compromised by an unwillingness to say the uncomfortable thing.
